Avoiding
Hallucinations.

Ground the model in your data, or prepare for fiction. If it isn't in context, it isn't knowledge.

⏱ 10 min◆ 3 interactives✦ +60 XP

Hallucination is not a bug. It's Claude doing its job — completing plausibly — with no ground truth to complete against. The fix isn't asking nicely. The fix is structural: give it the data, require citations, and allow an honest "I don't know."

If a fact isn't in context, treat it as fiction until proven otherwise.

The three grounding moves

01 · paste it

Direct grounding. Paste the doc, the log, the PR description. Then ask about that. "According to the attached…"

02 · cite it

Tell Claude: "Cite the exact line or quote for every claim. If you can't cite, say so." You get citations — and honest gaps.

03 · refuse mode

"If the answer isn't in the provided context, respond exactly: NOT_IN_CONTEXT." Gives you a clean signal instead of a guess.

Smell test: how to spot a hallucination

green flags
Quotes from context with line numbers.
"I can't find this in the attached docs."
Acknowledgements of uncertainty.
red flags
Oddly specific numbers with no source.
Named people/projects that don't appear in context.
Confident plural claims ("studies show…").
