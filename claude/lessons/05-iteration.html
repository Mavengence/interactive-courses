Iterative Prompting.

First drafts are for calibration, not delivery. The second prompt is always better — if you know what to change.

⏱ 10 min◆ 3 interactives✦ +60 XP

Beginners write one prompt and judge Claude on the output. Experienced users write a prompt, read the output, and use what they learned to refine the prompt. The model doesn't change. Your prompt does.

Anthropic's own engineers say it flatly: prompt engineering is a science — approach it like a scientist. Test, iterate, compare. The best first prompt you'll ever write is the one you wrote yesterday and are improving today.

Prompting is a loop, not a shot.

The three-turn loop

turn 1 · calibrate

Write a reasonable prompt. Read the output. Don't judge — diagnose. What did it misunderstand? What did it over-explain? What tone did it default to?

turn 2 · correct

Name the specific thing to fix. "Shorter." "Drop the intro paragraph." "Use the voice from the attached doc." Keep the rest of the prompt stable — change one variable at a time.

turn 3 · lock

When it's good: capture the full, working prompt somewhere (CLAUDE.md, a snippet file, Notion). That's now a template. Next time, start from there.

▸
Pro move Anthropic uses. After an output you liked, ask Claude: "Write the prompt you would have given yourself to produce this output." You now have a distilled, reusable prompt — often better than the one you started with.

Show, don't tell

The single highest-leverage iteration move: replace descriptions with examples. Saying "use a professional tone" gives Claude a fuzzy target. Pasting three sentences in the exact tone you want gives it coordinates.

Anthropic's prompt-improver workflow does this automatically — it takes your prompt and looks for places to inject 2–5 input/output examples. In their experience, examples are the single most reliable way to drive accuracy and consistency, especially for structured outputs. When your turn-2 correction is "make it feel more like X," paste X.

What to say in turn 2

Most failed iterations come from vague corrections. Here's the vocabulary that actually works:

do
"Cut the first paragraph."
"Use the voice from this example: …"
"Make the bullets parallel — each should start with a verb."
"Assume the reader already knows X; skip the intro."
don't
"Make it better."
"Less AI-sounding."
"Sharper."
"You know what I mean."

Try the loop

Ask for something. Read what comes back. Then — without starting a new prompt — iterate. The turn-2 correction is the muscle we're training.
