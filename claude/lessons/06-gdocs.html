Claude for Perfect
Google Docs.

From bullet dump to crisp doc in under five minutes. The doc is the artifact. The prompt is the mold.

⏱ 8 min◆ 3 interactives✦ +55 XP

The shared doc is a team's native thinking surface. Design reviews, PRDs, post-mortems, launch briefs — they all live there. A doc Claude writes well is one you can paste and polish, not one you have to rewrite.

Three moves do most of the work: give it the skeleton, show it the voice, ask for a critique before a rewrite.

Move 1 — Give it the skeleton

Gdocs have conventions. Design docs have TL;DRs. PRDs have success metrics. Post-mortems have timelines. If you don't hand Claude the skeleton, you'll get a generic "article."

Output structure:
# Title
## TL;DR (3 bullets, each <15 words)
## Context
## Proposal
## Risks & mitigations
## Success metrics
## Open questions

Move 2 — Give it the voice

Good internal docs have a voice: short sentences, active verbs, no marketing language, numbers before adjectives. If you want that voice, paste two paragraphs of an existing doc that has it, and say "match this voice."

★
Voice transfer works. Pasting 100-300 words of well-written reference text is worth more than 10 adjectives describing the voice you want.

Move 3 — Ask for the critique before the rewrite

After the first draft, resist the urge to immediately ask for a better version. Instead ask: "What are the three weakest parts of this doc, and why?" You'll get a better doc by fixing specific weaknesses than by asking for a "better version" — vague corrections produce vague outputs.

This is chain-of-thought applied to your own writing. You're forcing Claude to diagnose before it prescribes.
